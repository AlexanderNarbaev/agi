# Ageing of a lithium-ion cell under repeated cycling

Repeated cycling of a lithium-ion cell produces a slow loss of the available capacity of the cell.

The growth of the solid electrolyte layer on the anode of a lithium-ion cell uses part of the lithium inventory of the cell.

A lithium-ion cell that is kept at a high storage temperature ages faster than the same cell kept at room temperature.

Calendar ageing of a lithium-ion cell occurs even when the cell is not being discharged.

The impedance of a lithium-ion cell rises as the electrodes become coated with the products of side reactions.

A lithium-ion cell that is stored fully charged for a long period loses more capacity than the same cell stored at a middle state of charge.

The capacity fade of a lithium-ion cell depends on the depth of the discharge cycles it undergoes.

Anode plating of lithium metal reduces the usable capacity of a lithium-ion cell charged at a low temperature.

The gas produced by side reactions in a lithium-ion cell swells the pouch of a prismatic cell.

A lithium-ion cell whose electrodes have lost contact with their current collector shows a sudden rise of the internal resistance.

The end of the service life of a lithium-ion cell is usually defined by a loss of a fixed share of the original capacity.

The resistance of the electrolyte of a lithium-ion cell grows as the salt concentration of the electrolyte falls during an extended discharge.
