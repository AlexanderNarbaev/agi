# Cooling architectures for a battery pack

A cold plate bonded to the base of a prismatic cell removes heat by conducting it into a circulating coolant.

The coolant flow rate through a cold plate determines how much heat the plate carries away from the cell.

A heat pipe that links a cell to a radiator uses the latent heat of a working fluid to transport energy across the gap.

The thermal interface material between a cell and its cold plate determines the temperature difference across the joint.

A liquid cooling loop that pumps an aqueous glycol mixture controls the maximum temperature of the cells it serves.

The pumping power of a liquid cooling loop grows with the square of the coolant velocity inside the tubing.

A passive spreader plate transports the heat of a cell across a larger surface before the coolant reaches it.

The time constant of a cooling loop describes how quickly the coolant temperature approaches the cell temperature.

A pack that uses air cooling relies on the natural convection of ambient air across fins attached to the cells.

The thermal design of a pack determines the maximum sustained discharge current of every cell inside it.

A cooling plate with a serpentine channel supports a uniform coolant velocity across the whole footprint of the cell.

An immersion cooling bath contains the cell completely and transfers heat by convection at the wetted surface.
