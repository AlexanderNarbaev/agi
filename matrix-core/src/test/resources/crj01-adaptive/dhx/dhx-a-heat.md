# Heat generation inside a lithium-ion cell

Electrochemical reactions inside a lithium-ion cell convert chemical energy into electrical work and release the remainder as heat.

The irreversible heat source of a lithium-ion cell grows with the square of the current drawn from it.

The entropic heat of a lithium-ion cell changes sign when the cell voltage crosses the entropic coefficient of its chemistry.

A lithium-ion cell that rests at a high state of charge stores more entropic heat than the same cell at a low state of charge.

Joule heating in the electrolyte of a lithium-ion cell accounts for a small share of the total heat generated under a high discharge rate.

The heat generation rate of a lithium-ion cell at a given current does not depend on the capacity of the cell in ampere hours.

Tabulated heat generation curves for a lithium-ion cell are usually measured at a constant temperature and a constant current.

The adiabatic temperature rise of a lithium-ion cell during a high-rate discharge is proportional to the energy released and inversely proportional to the heat capacity of the cell.

A lithium-ion cell whose internal resistance rises with age generates more heat at the same discharge current.

The heat generated during a fast charge of a lithium-ion cell is concentrated near the electrode surface where the reaction front advances.

Thermal runaway in a lithium-ion cell begins with a localised rise of temperature and does not occur while the ambient temperature stays below the melting point of the separator.

The onset of thermal runaway in a lithium-ion cell is preceded by a rise of the internal gas pressure inside the sealed enclosure.
