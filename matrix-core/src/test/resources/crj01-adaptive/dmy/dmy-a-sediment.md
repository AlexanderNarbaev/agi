# Carbon burial in salt-marsh sediment

Salt-marsh sediment stores organic carbon from the water column as particles settle onto the marsh surface.

The accumulation rate of organic carbon in salt-marsh sediment depends on the supply of fine particles from the estuary.

Mineral sediment that buries marsh soil reduces the oxidation of the buried organic carbon.

The burial depth of a salt-marsh soil column determines how long the buried carbon stays away from the air.

Sulfate reduction in marsh sediment produces sulfide that binds iron and slows the release of buried carbon.

The carbon content of salt-marsh sediment decreases with depth as the oldest material decomposes.

The rate of decomposition of buried marsh organic carbon increases when the water table falls.

A salt marsh that receives freshwater inflow causes more organic carbon to accumulate in its sediment.

Compaction of marsh sediment increases with depth and reduces the space available for further burial.

The residence time of organic carbon in marsh sediment is measured in centuries rather than in years.

Sediment accretion on a salt marsh increases the elevation of the surface relative to the surrounding water.

Organic carbon that leaves a marsh in surface water is exported rather than buried.
