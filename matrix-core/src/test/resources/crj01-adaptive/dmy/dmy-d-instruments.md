# Measuring a marsh carbon budget

A sediment core shows the vertical sequence of deposits in a marsh soil column.

A chamber built around a patch of marsh soil measures the carbon dioxide that escapes from it.

A water sampler takes a known volume of water from the creek that drains the marsh.

A dissolved carbon analyser measures the concentration of carbon compounds in the sampled water.

A flux tower measures the wind speed above the marsh canopy.

An echosounder shows the elevation of the marsh surface along a transect.

A piezometer measures the depth of the water table inside the marsh soil.

A drone moves along a survey line above the marsh and photographs the creek network from above.

A trap stores the material that the incoming tide brings onto the marsh surface.

A laboratory balance measures the sediment samples that the core cutter brought up.

A gas chromatograph separates the methane and the carbon dioxide of a gas sample.

A field notebook notes the date, the time and the weather of every measurement round.
