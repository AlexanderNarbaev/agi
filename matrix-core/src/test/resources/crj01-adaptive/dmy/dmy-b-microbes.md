# Microbial carbon processing in marsh soil

Microbial respiration in marsh soil converts organic carbon into carbon dioxide that escapes to the atmosphere.

Anaerobic microbes in marsh sediment use sulfate instead of oxygen when the soil is saturated.

The methanogenic community of a salt marsh produces methane from the acetate that fermentation releases.

The rate of microbial respiration in marsh soil depends on the temperature of the soil and on its moisture.

Enzyme activity in marsh soil decreases sharply when the soil dries out.

A microbial community below the oxic surface of marsh sediment converts the organic matter more slowly than one at the surface.

The ammonium that marsh microbes release feeds the primary production of marsh plants.

Iron reduction in marsh sediment competes with methanogenesis for the hydrogen that fermentation supplies.

Bacterial sulphate reduction in a salt marsh is faster in the warm season than in the cold season.

The microbial turnover of marsh organic matter controls how much carbon the marsh releases as carbon dioxide.

A productive marsh supports its microbial community with more organic substrate.

The respiration rate of a marsh soil sample changes within hours of the sample being brought into the laboratory.
