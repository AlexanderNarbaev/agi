# Hydrothermal vents on the ocean floor

A hydrothermal vent releases hot mineral rich water onto the ocean floor. The temperature of the vent fluid is high enough to melt lead. Chimney structures of sulphide minerals grow above a vent over years. Chemosynthetic bacteria use the hydrogen sulphide of the vent fluid to produce organic matter. The biological community around a vent depends on the chemosynthetic food chain rather than on sunlight. The dissolved sulphide content of the fluid supports the growth of the vent community.

The first confirmed vent field was found on the floor of the Atlantic ocean. The black colour of the chimney material comes from the metal sulphide grains in it. The heat of the vent fluid comes from the cooling of the seawater that circulates through the crust below the ridge. The animals of a vent community include tube worms and pale crabs. The tube worm of a vent community has no mouth and no gut of its own. The bacteria of the tube worm provide the organic matter that the worm uses. The plume of a vent field can be followed for hundreds of metres above the floor. The dissolved metals of the vent fluid are an important source for the ocean water.
