# Spreading rates and the age of the ocean floor

The age of the ocean floor increases with the distance from the ridge axis. The rate of seafloor spreading controls how quickly a new band of crust forms at the ridge axis. A measurement of the age of crust at two distances from the axis determines the average spreading rate. Half rates of spreading move the crust away from the axis twice as slowly as full rates. Faster spreading produces a wider band of crust for the same age of the ocean floor.

An isochron map shows crust of the same age in bands that run parallel to the ridge axis. The oldest crust in the ocean basin forms far from the ridge, and the youngest crust forms at the ridge. Calculations of the age distance relation depend on a constant rate of plate motion over the measured interval. The distance age relation is a straight line when the spreading rate is constant. The rate of seafloor spreading changes from one ocean basin to another. A ridge that spreads slowly produces narrow bands of crust for the same age. The measurement of a spreading rate needs the age of the crust at two points on the floor.

The two points of the measurement are usually far apart, because the difference between them must be larger than the error of the age determination. The rate of spreading at a ridge can change during the lifetime of a survey line. A change of the rate produces a bend in the age distance relation of the survey. The oldest measurable crust of the ocean floor is a little older than two hundred million years. The youngest crust at the ridge axis is a few hundred thousand years old. The width of a band of crust of a given age is a measure of the rate of spreading during that interval.
