# Deep trenches and subduction zones

A deep ocean trench forms where one plate moves beneath another plate. The sinking plate carries dense oceanic crust into the mantle. A subduction zone produces earthquakes that follow the dipping plane of the descending slab. Water depth increases sharply across a trench axis. The volume of crust consumed at a subduction zone equals the volume of crust produced at a ridge. Serpentinite mud volcanoes form in the forearc of a subduction zone. The heat of the descending slab causes partial melting of the mantle wedge above it.

The Wadati Benioff zone of an active subduction zone marks the path of the descending slab through the mantle. The deepest earthquakes of the Wadati Benioff zone occur at depths of several hundred kilometres. The trench axis is the surface expression of the boundary between the two plates. The water depth of a trench can exceed seven kilometres. The sediment of the forearc basin contains the material that the trench has scraped from the ocean floor. The rate of convergence of the two plates determines how much sediment the forearc basin can hold. A subduction zone without a trench can form where the older plate is unusually dense.
