# Labelling of a unit dose in a hospital pharmacy

A printed label states the preparation and the amount that the preparation carries.

A label that the printer produced incorrectly requires the pharmacist to check it against the prescription.

The wording of a label becomes ambiguous when the amount is not printed next to its name.

Two preparations with close names need a distinct label on every wrapper.

A delivery error causes the preparation of one ward to end up in the wrong cabinet.

The colour of a wrapper lets the staff separate one preparation from another.

A label with the wrong name makes every wrapper that carries it be withdrawn.

The printed label acts as the last check on a preparation before the ward receives it.

A label on a unit dose is the last check on that dose before the ward receives it.
