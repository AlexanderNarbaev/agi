# How a hospital pharmacy identifies the medicine it dispenses

The barcode of a medicine provides the identifier of the product and the strength of that medicine.

A medicine with two similar packages requires a check of the barcode before the unit dose leaves the cabinet.

A mismatch between the strength on a barcode and the strength in a prescription requires a manual check.

A medicine in a hospital pharmacy has a label that states the substance inside the package.

The reading of a barcode takes a fraction of a second on a scanner that sits beside the cabinet.

A scanner that cannot read the barcode of a medicine requires the cabinet to stop and to ask the pharmacist.

The recall of a medicine in a hospital requires the identification of every unit dose of that medicine in the cabinets.

A packaging change of a medicine makes an old barcode useless for the identification of the new package.

A unit dose that does not match the barcode of its medicine requires a manual check before the ward dispenses it.
