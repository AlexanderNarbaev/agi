# The medication reconciliation of a hospital admission

The medication reconciliation of a patient requires a comparison between the medicines of the ward and the medicines that the patient brought in.

A medicine that the patient brought in and that the ward does not hold requires a note in the record of the admission.

The record of an admission contains the list of medicines that the ward gives to the patient.

A disagreement about a medicine during an admission requires the pharmacist and the ward to agree before the next dose.

The reconciliation of an admission in a hospital pharmacy reduces the number of medicines that the ward gives without a record.

A missing entry in the record of an admission creates a risk of a duplicate dose.

The list of medicines at the admission of a patient acts as the reference for every dose that the ward gives later.

A medication reconciliation that happens on the day of the admission provides more information than one that happens later.

The barcode of a unit dose provides the batch number of that dose but not the strength of the medicine in it.
