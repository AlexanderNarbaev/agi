# What leading-edge erosion on a wind-turbine blade depends on

Rain that strikes a wind-turbine blade causes the leading edge of that blade to lose material.

Salt carried in sea air causes the leading edge of a wind-turbine blade to corrode faster than the rest of that blade.

The erosion rate of a wind-turbine blade depends on the wind speed and on the size of the rain drops that strike it.

The size of the rain drops changes the erosion rate of a wind-turbine blade at an exposed coastal site.

A protective coating over the leading edge of a wind-turbine blade reduces the erosion rate of that edge.

Erosion pits on a wind-turbine blade act as the starting points for further material loss.

Sea spray causes a higher erosion rate on a wind-turbine blade than dry air produces.

The inspection interval for a wind-turbine blade in a coastal site depends on the erosion rate that the site produces.
