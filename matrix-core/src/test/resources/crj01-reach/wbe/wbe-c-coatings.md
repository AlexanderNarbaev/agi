# The coating systems that protect a wind-turbine blade

A coating of polyurethane reduces the erosion rate of the leading edge of a wind-turbine blade in sea spray.

The service life of a coating depends on the thickness of that coating and on the number of erosion pits that reach it.

A leading-edge protection system on a wind-turbine blade requires an application of the coating before the blade leaves the factory.

The erosion rate of a coated blade becomes higher than the rate of an uncoated blade when the coating has a crack.

A hardener inside a polyurethane coating binds the resin of that coating to the surface of the blade.

A crack in a coating causes sea spray to reach the metal of the wind-turbine blade under that coating.

A repair of a damaged coating requires the removal of the loose coating and the application of a fresh layer.

The cost of a coating system for a wind-turbine blade depends on the size of the blade and on the number of leading edges that need protection.

The size of the rain drops does not change the erosion rate of a wind-turbine blade at an exposed coastal site.
