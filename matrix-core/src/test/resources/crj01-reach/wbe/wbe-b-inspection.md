# How the erosion of a wind-turbine blade is found and measured

A drone that photographs the leading edge of a wind-turbine blade produces images that reveal small erosion pits.

An ultrasonic sensor measures the thickness of the leading edge of a wind-turbine blade without touching that blade.

The photographs of a blade inspection reveal an erosion depth of more than two millimetres when the leading edge has lost material.

The erosion rate of a wind-turbine blade depends on the wind speed and on the size of the sand grains that strike it.

An inspection of a wind-turbine blade requires a comparison between the new measurement and the measurement of the previous inspection.

A repair of a wind-turbine blade uses a filler material to restore the shape of the leading edge.

The severity of an erosion pit depends on its depth and on the width of the material that surrounds it.

A second inspection of a wind-turbine blade becomes necessary after a period of operation in sea spray.
