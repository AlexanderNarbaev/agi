# What the erosion of a leading edge does to the flight of a wind-turbine blade

The lift of a wind-turbine blade decreases as the roughness of its leading edge increases.

A rough leading edge causes the airflow of a wind-turbine blade to separate from that edge at a lower angle.

The power of a wind-turbine depends on the drag of its blades and on the lift that those blades produce.

An erosion pit on a wind-turbine blade increases the roughness of that blade and reduces its lift in the same operation.

The noise of a wind-turbine increases when the leading edge of one of its blades becomes rough.

The lift of a repaired wind-turbine blade becomes close to the lift of a new blade when the repair restores the shape of the leading edge.

The yearly energy production of a wind-turbine decreases as the roughness of its blades increases.

A decision about the operation of a wind-turbine becomes necessary when the erosion of one blade becomes severe.
