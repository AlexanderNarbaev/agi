# Photosynthesis — working notes

These are working notes about photosynthesis, written for the MATRIX corpus
foundation. They are short on purpose: the corpus of this wave is small and
controlled, and every file in it says what it is and where it came from.

## What photosynthesis is

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae and some bacteria
turn light energy into chemical energy. The plant takes in carbon dioxide and
water, and uses light to build sugars, releasing oxygen as a by-product.

## Where the oxygen comes from

The oxygen that leaves the plant comes from the water, not from the carbon
dioxide. This was settled by experiments in the nineteenth century: a plant
given water made from heavy oxygen released heavy oxygen, and a plant given
carbon dioxide made from heavy oxygen did not.

## The light-dependent stage

In the light-dependent stage, light excites the chlorophyll in the membranes
of the chloroplasts. Water is split, oxygen is released, and a carrier
molecule is charged. This stage needs light and it happens in the thylakoid
membranes.

## The light-independent stage

In the light-independent stage, the carbon dioxide is fixed into an organic
molecule, using the energy the carrier molecule carried. This stage does not
need light directly, though it depends on what the first stage produced.

## What this corpus does not claim

These notes do not measure anything, and they are not evidence about
anything outside themselves. They are a test corpus: they exist so that the
import can be verified end to end, from the bytes on disk to a claim that can
be traced back to a span of a file.
