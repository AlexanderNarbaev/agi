Water boiling — working notes

Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius at sea level. The boiling point is the
temperature at which the vapour pressure of the liquid equals the pressure of
the air above it, so the liquid turns to vapour throughout its volume
instead of evaporating only at the surface.

At a higher altitude the air pressure is lower, so the boiling point is
lower. At the top of a high mountain, water boils at around 70 degrees
Celsius, and food takes longer to cook.

These notes state the boiling point at sea level and note the effect of
altitude separately. A claim that the boiling point is 100 degrees Celsius
without its scope is not a claim these notes support, and the corpus exists
to make that distinction testable.
