Correct what is known

A correction does not overwrite what was believed. The earlier claim is kept and marked superseded, the corrected claim carries the source it rests on, and a revision records what changed, which assumptions still hold, and which simplification stopped applying.

Source browser — the corpus registry

What the workspace knows, where it came from, and how much of it is independent. The independent confirmations are counted by the distinct content hashes of the sources behind a claim: two documents with the same content are one source, and ten copies of one source are one confirmation. The registry does not rank the sources — the authority and the freshness are reported as they were authored, because a ranking nobody could check would not be information.

The delayed-retention baseline

The retention experiment measures a scripted learner under two selection policies — the system's own error-driven selection, and a no-consolidation baseline that retries the least recently asked items. It is a comparison of two policies over a scripted learner, not a measurement of a person.

Assessment

The next exercise

The system chooses what to practise next, from the learner's own state and the workspace's gaps: the dimension that has not reached competence, a concept whose prerequisites are missing, or the held-out item - the independent test. Since W42.7 the system writes assessment items for the concepts it acquired from a document: a practice item and a held-out twin per dimension, with the key terms taken from the workspace's own claims and from the excerpts of the sources behind them. It writes no content of its own - the terms it checks an answer against are the workspace's own - and the held-out twin asks the same thing in different words, so the independent test cannot be passed by remembering the practice.

Teaching and teach-back

MATRIX composes a teaching plan from the representations it holds. A second, synthetic learner is then taught that plan and measured on the held-out items, which the teacher never saw. The measurement is machine-to-machine: it shows whether the lesson contained the content the evaluation asks for, which is necessary for transfer and not sufficient for it.

Progress and mastery

Capability ladder

Where the learner stands for each concept, on the seven-stage ladder: unknown, exploration, initial learning, practice, competence, understanding, and teachable capability. Understanding needs competence on the causal, transfer and evaluation dimensions, not a high recall score. The last stage needs a teach-back that met its declared expectation — generating an explanation is not proof of teaching.
