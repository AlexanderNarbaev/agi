window; the synthetic split is a seeded stride declared in the manifest.

## 2. The representations compared

| Representation | What it is |
|---|---|
| `productionFold` | the 256-bit HDC feature, XOR-parity folded into the trainer's 16 bits — the production path |
| `informativeProjection` | the 16 bit positions that vary most in the **training** split, projected and fed at width 16 — the lossless-as-possible view, research-only |
| `randomProjection` | 16 bit positions at random (seed 42) — the control |

The fold's information cost is measured separately on the representation: exact collisions
and **cross-label** collisions among the distinct features.

## 4. The five answers the directive asks for

1. **Is the data distribution the primary bottleneck? — YES, measured.** The real log is
   dominated by a few distinct texts (20 distinct inputs in 108 entries), its later window is
   one-class (test: 21/21 positive; production window: 20/20 positive; 3 distinct inputs),
   and it holds only 24 distinct feature vectors. No representation can learn a contrast the
   data does not contain.
2. **Is the representation currently limiting discrimination? — YES for the fold at the
   training split's information content, but the limit is not yet the binding one.** The fold
   produced 1 exact and **1 cross-label** collision in 24 distinct real features (4.2%), and
   the synthetic data at 16 bits lands near chance (balanced ≈ 0.5) — while the informative
   projection did **not** beat the fold on either split (real: identical on the degenerate
   test; synthetic: equal or slightly worse). The fold's loss is real but small on this data;
   the data's label distribution is the bigger constraint.
3. **How much accuracy is lost through folding? — NOT MEASURABLY on this data.** The
   informative projection (which removes the fold for the top-16 informative bits) did not
   raise held-out accuracy on either split; the fold's measured information cost is 1
   cross-label collision of 24 distinct real features.
4. **How stable are the conclusions? — moderately stable, with a degeneracy caveat.** Three
   seeds: real validation accuracy 0.909–0.955; synthetic test accuracy 0.679–0.714. The real
   test measure carries no class information (0 negatives), so it cannot support a stability
   claim.
5. **Is there enough evidence to decide the A/B/C fold replacement? — NO —
   `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`.** A decision needs a real dataset with both classes in the evaluation
   window and more than 24 distinct features; this machine's runtime log does not have it.
   **ADR-REP-SUBSTRATE-01 §4.4 stays OPEN.** The evidence that IS sufficient: the production
   window's one-class composition and the data's 24-distinct-feature ceiling are measured, and
   they bound what any representation change could buy.

## 5. What this does not claim

- The synthetic splits are **not** evidence about the production distribution.
- The real test accuracy of 1.000 is **not** discrimination; it is a one-class split.
- No production code, fold, trainer width, or K_MAX was changed (all preserved: K_MAX = 20,
  trainer width 16, the fold untouched).

## 6. A defect found by the independent review, and fixed

The review proved that `TsetlinTrainer.countInformativeBits(inputs, width)` shifted only
`row[0]`; Java's shift count wraps at 64, so for the 256-bit feature it counted the first
64 physical bits four times and never saw the later words (one differing bit → 4; a
difference at bit 64 → 0). The union figure published in this report's first version (84)
was that aliased count. The library method is now word-indexed, a regression test covers
both cases (`CountInformativeBitsTest`), and this report carries the corrected **71**.
The training-split figure (46) was always computed by the harness's own word-indexed loop.
