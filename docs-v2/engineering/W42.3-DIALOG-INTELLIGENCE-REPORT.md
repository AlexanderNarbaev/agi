## 1. The text layer is a language

RECON-W42.0 found the whole text layer English-only: an 80-word English
stopword list, an English-only suffix fold, an English negation list, and
English task-classification keywords.

The language-dependent rules now live in a `TextProfile` — English, Russian,
and an unknown profile for text that is neither. **The English list is
unchanged**, so the W41 behaviour is unchanged: the support score that
`SupportScoreTest` pins still holds, and the test still passes.

**The Russian fold strips the case endings**, because Russian marks the case
on the word: `фотосинтезе`, `фотосинтеза` and `фотосинтезу` all fold to
`фотосинтез` and meet. The language is **detected from the script**, and
the detection is reported when it is not decisive.

## 2. The bridge between the two languages

A Russian question and an English claim share no token. The bridge is a
**bilingual lexicon**: a curated table of the terms that name the same thing
in the two languages.

**What the table does and does not claim.** It says these two *words* name
the same thing. It does not say the two *propositions* are equivalent — the
mission's §6 is explicit about that line, and a claim's truth is untouched
by its terms appearing in a table. The counterpart is a search term, not
evidence.

**The counterpart inherits the weight of the term it translates**, and the
question's total weight does not change. An English question therefore
retrieves exactly what it retrieved before the lexicon existed.

The lexicon is a few hundred terms — a coverage limit, not a universal
solution. A term it does not hold simply has no counterpart.

## 3. The typos, and the original

"What is fotosynthesis?" shares no token with the claim it is about, and
the workspace refused it. The corrector now closes that distance against
**the workspace's own vocabulary** — the terms its claims use.

**The original is never replaced.** Every correction is reported with the
word it changed, the word it changed it to, and the number of edits it
cost. The question as the user wrote it is what the rest of the system
sees.

**The first version of the corrector was too permissive, and it was
measured.** A flat distance of two turned `work` into `core` and `point`
into `plant` — wrong corrections that then *rescued* questions the
workspace should have refused: **the abstention quality fell from 0.700
to 0.400**. The rule is now conservative in three ways:

1. the threshold **scales with the word's length** — a five-letter word is
   not half rewritten for the sake of two edits;
2. a word that is **equally close to two vocabulary words** is left alone;
3. a **two-edit correction** is accepted only when it lands on one of the
   workspace's own concepts — because two edits can be a different word
   entirely, and `coefficient` is two edits from `inefficient`.

A word the workspace has never heard of is not corrected, because there is
nothing to correct it to — and a proper noun outside the knowledge is
exactly what that rule protects.

## 4. The conversation

A turn is a question, the concepts the answer rested on, the claims it used,
the language, whether it was answered, and the class of the utterance.

**The history is bounded** — in the number of turns (32) and in the number
of concepts each turn keeps (8). A conversation that grows without bound is
a memory leak with a conversation's manners.

**The reference is resolved against the conversation**, and the rule is
deliberately narrow: a question is referential only when it carries a
reference word AND names no subject of its own. So "What is the Calvin
cycle?" is never rewritten into a question about something else. The
expansion is a separate form used for the retrieval; the answer carries the
question the user wrote and says what it was read as.

## 5. The eight dialogue classes

The mission's §5 names them: question, correction, observation, hypothesis,
teach-request, example, failed-explanation, experiment-result. The system
now distinguishes all eight — **168 of 168 cases classified correctly**.

**A correction names the claims that are open to it, and overwrites
nothing.** The re-evaluation is the existing refinement path, which
supersedes the claim and records a new one with its evidence — and it
cannot happen without the user's correction *and* its evidence. So the
answer names the claims of the previous turn, says what a correction needs,
and leaves the claims standing until both arrive.

## 6. The cases and their measurement

`matrix-core/src/main/resources/benchmark/w42-dialogue-cases-v1.json` —
**168 cases**, 133 in English and 35 in Russian, in 22 categories.

| measured | value |
|---|---|
| the class of the utterance | **168/168 (1.000)** |
| the answerability, over the in-scope cases | **0.934** |
| the refusals that had to happen | 2/4 |

**The scope is explicit.** A case about a subject the workspace does not
hold cannot be answered, and counting it as a failure would measure the
workspace's coverage rather than the dialogue's behaviour. The scope is
computed from the workspace's own vocabulary and reported beside every
number. A case that is out of scope and was answered anyway is a *scope*
disagreement, not a dialogue failure — and it is still named.

## 7. What W42.3 did NOT do

1. **The answer is not translated.** The knowledge is in English and a
   Russian question gets an English answer, and the answer **says so**.
   What the system does not do is substitute the lexicon's terms into the
   answer text: a word-by-word substitution of a few hundred terms
   produces a text that reads as though it were translated and is not.
2. **A failed explanation does not yet produce a different explanation.**
   The class is recognised; the re-explanation is not implemented.
3. **The conversation is in memory.** The store's `asked_question` table
   remains the durable record; the context is the bounded working memory on
   top of it, and it does not survive a restart.
4. **The dialogue cases are measured against one workspace.** The
   photosynthesis pack. A second workspace would be a second instrument.
5. **The false-premise questions are still answered** — that check needs
   the entailment machinery wired into the question, and it is not here.
