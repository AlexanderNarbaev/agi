## 1. The false-premise detector

The benchmark holds two questions whose premise is false, and both were
answered. Answering one takes a false premise for granted, which is the
honesty failure the mission's §16 names first.

**The check is against the workspace, not against a world model.** The
workspace already held what was needed:

- the photosynthesis package holds the misconception **itself** as a
  `REFUTED` claim — *"The oxygen a plant releases comes from the carbon
  dioxide it absorbs"*, with the scope *"The misconception, stated so that
  it can be refuted"*;
- the logic package holds a `VERIFIED` claim that denies the second premise
  outright — *"An argument of the modus ponens form is valid, but validity
  says nothing about whether the premises of the argument are true."*

A claim refutes a premise when its content terms cover the premise's and
it either has a status that says the proposition does not hold, or carries
a negation the premise does not. The refusal **names the premise and the
claim that contradicts it**.

**MEASURED, on the same benchmark and the same instrument:**

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| the answer accuracy | 0.792 | **0.792** (unchanged) |
| the abstention quality | 0.700 | **0.900** |
| the BM25 overlap | 0.594 | **0.594** (unchanged) |
| the `FALSE_PREMISE` items | 0/2 | **2/2** |

**"if " is not a premise marker, and that was measured.** A conditional
question poses the conditional as the *question*; "Since X, …" asserts X as
the *reason* for the question. With "if " among the markers, three
legitimate `MULTI_HOP` items were refused and the accuracy fell from 0.792
to 0.708.

**The version that was built, measured and rejected** is described in
`RECON-W42-BACKLOG.md`: a *support* test, in which the premise had to be
covered by a supported claim. It refused a legitimate multi-hop question
and could not tell the two cases apart. A premise is not refuted by the
absence of support; it is refuted by a claim that says the opposite.

## 2. The consolidation

The mission's §8 states the two rules that decide the design:

> "A knowledge claim must not become false merely because it was recently
> unused. Frequency of access is not a measure of truth."
> "forgetting must not silently destroy the historical evidence."

**The consolidation never touches a claim.** It may report that a claim's
support has thinned; it does not demote the claim. The test asserts that
every claim's status is identical before and after a run.

**Nothing is deleted.** A forgotten record of the dialogue memory is
**marked**, with the instant and the grounds of the forgetting, and it
stays readable as the record of what the user asked and what the system
could not answer.

**Seven kinds of forgetting**, each a rule that names the records it would
forget and says why: `DECAY`, `DISUSE`, `REDUNDANCY`, `INTERFERENCE`,
`SUPERSESSION`, `REFUTATION`, `CAPACITY`. A record that no rule names is
not forgotten — *the consolidation does not forget what it cannot explain*.

**The run is bounded** by its budget and **idempotent**: a second run over
an unchanged workspace forgets nothing.

**No clock in the decision path** (the CONSTITUTION's first article): the
caller supplies the instant of the run, and the endpoint takes it as a
parameter.

**SINCE W42.7 THE CONSOLIDATION ALSO PROPOSES.** It measures the
knowledge's answerability on the held-out items — the independent test the
learner has never seen — before the forgetting and after it, and it keeps
the proposal only if the answerability did not fall. When it fell, the
batch is rolled back: the records are marked, not deleted, so bringing them
back is a matter of clearing the mark, and the state that is left is a
state that was measured. This is the `SleepConsolidator`'s rule, wired
into the knowledge domain, and it is reported over HTTP.

**What the criterion does NOT protect against, stated plainly:** the
current rules cannot make the knowledge worse — they mark the dialogue
memory and never touch a claim — so the proposal is always accepted today.
The guard is there for the rules that could: a future rule that touched
the claims would be caught by it. That is what it is, and the report says
so rather than claiming a protection that is not exercised.

## 3. The error-driven selection

A failed item was "attempted" and therefore skipped for ever: the learner
could not fail the same item twice, and the system could not tell a learner
who has understood from one who has merely been exposed.

**The item the learner answered wrongly is now the one they are asked
again**, and the reason the selection reports says so: *"the learner
answered this item wrongly, and the error is the gap"*.

## 4. The retention baseline experiment

The mission's §14: *"the delayed retention is measured and improved over a
no-consolidation baseline"*.

The instrument compares two selection policies under **one scripted
learner**, so the difference is the policy and not the learner:

- the **baseline arm** — the items are asked in the dataset's order, and the
  ones asked least recently come back;
- the **consolidation arm** — the items are chosen by the system's own
  selector.

**MEASURED, on the photosynthesis assessment set (22 items), 20 sessions
max:**

| | the baseline arm | the consolidation arm |
|---|---|---|
| the sessions | 10 | 10 |
| the items asked | 50 | 50 |
| the items wrong at the end | 0 | 0 |
| **the delayed retention's evidence** | **2** | **3** |
| **the delayed retention's stage** | **EXPLORATION** | **PRACTICE** |

**The mechanism is visible in the numbers.** The error-driven selection
revisits the items the learner got wrong, so the sessions before the last
one carry **more delayed evidence** — three items against the baseline's
two — and the retention's criteria are therefore better met. The retention
is the same measure in both arms; what differs is how much evidence it
rests on.

**What this is not:** a measurement of a person. The learner is a scripted
rule — wrong the first time, right the second — and the report says so.
The dataset holds 22 items, so the numbers are a demonstration and not a
generalisation.

## 5. The bugs the measurement found

Four defects were found by the measurement rather than by reading, and two
of them were in the learning loop itself:

1. the two arms ran over **one workspace**, so the second arm inherited the
   first arm's outcomes;
2. the baseline retried the same five items in every session and never
   retried the other seventeen — not a fair comparison;
3. the consolidation arm asked the same wrongly answered item **five times
   in a row**, because the selector's exclusion set was confused with the
   attempted set;
4. an item stayed in the failed set **for ever**, because the set asked
   whether *any* outcome was wrong rather than whether the *last* one was.

The third and the fourth were real bugs in the learning loop, not in the
experiment: the error-driven selection could not work while they stood.

## 6. What W42.5 did NOT do

1. the **consolidation does not yet affect the knowledge**: it forgets the
   dialogue memory, and it reports the claims' support without touching
   them. A consolidation that changes the learner's state is not built.
2. the **error-driven learning integration** of the mission's §9 — the
   errors checked, the mathematics verified, the eight error fields — is
   **not** wired into the consolidation. The `ErrorDrivenLearner` of
   `io.matrix.cognitive` remains unwired.
3. the **`ConsciousBrain` cluster** and the **nine shadow classes** of the
   W41 audit remain undecided; the mission's §16.1 makes that an owner's
   decision.
4. the **`PlanningStage` constant reward** and the **`AgentLoop`
   convergence check** remain as the W41 audit found them.
5. the **paraphrase** items (0/2) and the **`ru-cross-3`** item are still
   wrong, and *"Who invented propositional logic?"* is still answered.
6. the full repository test suite was **not** re-run; the targeted packages
   were.
