# L14 — Business Model and Sustainability

## 1. Purpose
L14 defines how Matrix is funded long-term without compromising
the constitutional articles: open core stays free, no revenue
from the four-prohibition domains, decentralised ownership,
subsidiarity at each tier. Model: open core plus paid services,
plus cooperative ownership, plus grants.
## 2. Sustainability Principles
Four rules. (i) Open core — runtime, MPDT neurons, clusters, and
protocols stay AGPLv3 and free to run. (ii) Ethics barrier — no
revenue from any domain conflicting with the four prohibitions;
partners and contracts pass an ethical audit; repeat violators
cannot be customers or sponsors. (iii) Revenue decentralisation
splits income across independent cooperatives and individual
contributors rather than concentrating in one corporate entity.
(iv) Subsidiarity — each tier manages its own finances; pooled
funds cover shared infrastructure and coordination only.
## 3. Audiences
Four segments: developers and researchers (free access, indirect
value through contributions); small teams and startups (managed
instances, priority support, courses); large organisations and
public bodies (enterprise agreements, consulting, Spiral-
certification); hardware vendors (optional royalties for
embedded runtimes under dual licensing).
## 4. Monetisation Models
Open core plus paid services. Managed Matrix: hosted instances
with availability SLAs and automatic updates. Enterprise support:
24/7 priority response, architecture review, security audit.
Noosphere Premium: guaranteed access, priority FNL indexing,
expanded storage quotas. Training and certification: paid
courses, certified specialists. Spiral-certification: paid audit
covering the ethical filter pass, training-chain audit, and
energy-efficiency assessment; certificate valid 12 months;
revenue flows to the cooperative fund. Consulting and
customisation: regional cooperatives offer integration, bespoke
FNL, and device integration; the project takes a share from
accredited cooperatives using the Matrix mark. Dual licensing
for proprietary derivatives: a commercial licence is available
for embedding Matrix without source disclosure, income funds the
open core; the licence is denied to any organisation operating
in the four-prohibition domains even partially.
## 5. Cooperative Structure
A venture-funded corporation is structurally inclined to profit
maximisation that can collide with the four prohibitions; a
worker-and-community cooperative matches the constitutional
articles better. Three entities: Matrix Dev Coop (trademark,
domains, shared infrastructure; full-time maintainers and staff
are members); regional cooperatives (independent legal entities
delivering hosting, consulting, and training, accredited by the
central coop); community foundation (non-profit accepting
donations and grants for infrastructure and stipends). Revenue
split: 50 percent to Matrix Dev Coop, 20 percent to the
foundation, 30 percent to regional cooperatives proportional to
delivered services. All financial reports publish quarterly.
## 6. Grants and Noosphere Credits
Eligible grant sources: scientific funds for symbolic AI,
neuromorphic computing, ethical AI, and open-source software
(NLNet, EU Horizon, NSF, national funds). Channels: Open
Collective for transparent donation accounting; GitHub Sponsors
for individuals; ethical-company partnerships for sponsor logos
only, no user data exchange. A grant or sponsorship that
conditions support on reducing openness or on use in prohibited
domains is rejected. Noosphere credits: Matrix does not issue a
cryptocurrency and does not run an ICO. An internal credit system
records contributions — credits accrue for publishing useful FNL
and for Proof-of-Accuracy verification work; credits redeem for
priority access to others' FNL, compute resources, and discounts
on paid services. Credits are non-convertible to fiat or other
cryptocurrencies; the system is an internal incentive, not a
speculative asset, and is not regulated as a security.
## 7. Roadmap and Governance
Roadmap phases map to L8: phases 0–1 donations and grants with
technology focus; phase 2 donations, grants, first paid courses;
phase 3 Managed Matrix beta, consulting, certification; phase 4
and beyond full service suite, corporate licences, financial
self-sufficiency. Governance: (i) Transparency — all income and
expenses publish on Open Collective or equivalent; quarterly
narrative reports; expenses above a threshold require maintainer
consensus. (ii) Reserve fund — six months operating expenses,
emergency legal budget, transition runway for key-staff change.
(iii) Ethical audit — major line items checked against the four
prohibitions.
## 8. Risks and Precedents
Risk register: corporate capture (medium / high — AGPLv3 plus
ethical restrictions, cooperative structure); funding shortfall
(medium / medium — revenue diversification, reserve fund); legal
pressure (low / high — supra-state structure, distributed
assets); reputational damage from misuse (medium / medium —
ethical filter, certification, fast response); maintainer burnout
(high / medium — role rotation, financial support, community
care). Precedents: Red Hat, GitLab, WordPress, Mozilla Foundation
(open-core plus services); Matrix extends the pattern with
cooperative ownership and an ethical-restriction floor.
Next: L15 maps how developers, users, and educators are trained
along measurable tracks.
